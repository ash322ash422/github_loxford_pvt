This paper investigates how generative artificial intelligence affects real-world product markets by exploiting a rare natural experiment: the unexpected leak of a highly proficient anime-style image generation model. Using more than 190,000 order records from a Chinese art outsourcing marketplace, the study compares the anime-style painting market—directly impacted by the leaked AI—with the wallpaper market, which serves as a control.

The results contradict common fears that generative AI harms human creators. Although the emergence of AI significantly reduces the average price of commissions, it simultaneously leads to a substantial increase in the number of orders and overall revenue. Specifically, the anime market experiences a sharp decline in price but more than doubles in order volume, ultimately generating higher total earnings.

To explain this counterintuitive pattern, the authors use supply and demand theory. Generative AI slightly reduces demand, as some consumers can satisfy their needs independently, but it dramatically increases supply by improving artists’ productivity. This outward shift in supply dominates the inward shift in demand, producing lower prices but higher quantities and higher revenue overall.

The findings suggest that generative AI augments artistic production rather than replacing artists. Practitioners are encouraged to integrate AI into their workflows to improve efficiency and income. Policymakers should view generative AI as a driver of economic growth instead of a threat, and the AI research community can take these results as evidence that AI can positively impact creative industries.
