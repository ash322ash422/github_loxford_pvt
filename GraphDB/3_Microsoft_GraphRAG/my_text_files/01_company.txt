Meridian Health Systems, a hospital network based in Austin, Texas, announced a partnership with NovaCare Robotics in March 2024 to deploy surgical assistance robots across its twelve facilities. The deal was negotiated by Meridian's Chief Innovation Officer, Dr. Amara Osei, who had previously led a similar robotics initiative at Cedar Ridge Medical before joining Meridian in 2022. NovaCare Robotics, founded in 2018 by engineer Wei Zhang, had built its reputation supplying robotic systems to research hospitals in Boston before expanding into the broader commercial healthcare market.

The partnership faced early resistance from the Texas Nurses Coalition, which raised concerns about job displacement and patient safety during a public hearing in Austin. In response, Dr. Osei and Wei Zhang co-hosted a town hall at Meridian's flagship hospital, where they committed to a phased rollout and a retraining program for surgical staff. The retraining program was later adopted as a model by two other hospital networks, including Cedar Ridge Medical, Dr. Osei's former employer, which signed its own robotics agreement with NovaCare in early 2025.

By the end of 2025, NovaCare Robotics had become the leading supplier of surgical robots in the Southern United States, and Meridian Health Systems reported a measurable drop in average surgery times across its network. Wei Zhang was later invited to speak at the National Healthcare Innovation Summit in Chicago, where he credited the Austin partnership — and the retraining program born out of the nurses' objections — as the turning point that made hospital systems trust robotic surgery at scale.
